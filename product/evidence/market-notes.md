# Market and competitive notes

> **Provenance label: SYNTHETIC / DEMO-ONLY.** These are invented desk-research notes describing generic commerce patterns. They are not current claims about named marketplaces and do not use live pricing, seller, inventory, or feature data.

| ID | Synthetic observation | Possible implication | Counterpoint / uncertainty |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `MK-01` | Marketplaces often let shoppers save a query or favorite an item. | “Save this search” may be a recognizable mental model for repeat collectors. | Familiarity does not prove discoverability or value in HoloMart. |
| `MK-02` | Collectors use wishlists or binder lists for stable card intent. | A future list may complement dynamic Saved Searches. | Search intent and collection ownership are different data models. |
| `MK-03` | Search alerts commonly combine item criteria with a price threshold. | Saved criteria could become one input to an alert. | Saving is not consent, and sold inventory can make an alert immediately stale. |
| `MK-04` | Collectible marketplaces emphasize condition, seller reputation, and price context. | These signals should remain visible beside every listing. | More metadata can increase cognitive load and still cannot guarantee authenticity. |
| `MK-05` | Commerce products expose channel, frequency, pause, and unsubscribe settings. | Any price alert needs explicit preferences and lifecycle controls. | Notification infrastructure is expensive before demand is established. |
| `MK-06` | Catalog taxonomies and product availability change over time. | Saved-search recovery is foundational for trust. | We have no real benchmark for the best partial-apply pattern. |

## Tensions to keep visible

- Pattern familiarity favors a conventional Saved Searches panel, while low synthetic adoption may indicate weak placement or narrow demand.
- Account sync improves continuity but introduces durable data lifecycle obligations.
- Price alerts may increase value but require separate consent, freshness, and sold-inventory semantics.
- Listing trust signals aid decisions but do not replace authentication, grading, or buyer protection operations.

Use these notes with the [other evidence](README.md), not as a feature checklist.
